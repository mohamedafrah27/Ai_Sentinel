# Domain Impersonation and Brand Spoofing

## Techniques in Domain Impersonation
Domain impersonation involves registering or configuring domain names that closely resemble established, trusted brands to trick users during visual inspection.

### 1. Typosquatting (URL Hijacking)
Attackers register common misspellings of popular domains (e.g., `exampel.com` or `paypa1.com`). Users making typographical errors in their address bar or scanning deceptive text quickly can easily land on typosquatted domains.

### 2. Combosquatting
Combining a legitimate brand trademark with security, authentication, or technical terms such as `paypal-security-update.com`, `apple-id-verify.com`, or `microsoft-support-login.com`. This exploits the user's trust in security-sounding vocabulary.

### 3. Subdomain Deception
Attackers leverage multi-level subdomains where the target brand is positioned in the subdomain prefix (e.g., `paypal.com.account-update.xyz`). Unwary users read the beginning of the URL and assume the authority belongs to the brand rather than the root domain (`account-update.xyz`).

### 4. High-Abuse Top-Level Domains (TLDs)
Phishing operators frequently exploit cheap, anonymous, or high-abuse TLDs such as `.xyz`, `.top`, `.tk`, `.ml`, `.pw`, `.cc`, and `.club` because of lax registration oversight and low acquisition costs.
